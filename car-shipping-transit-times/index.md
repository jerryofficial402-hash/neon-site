## From Booking to Delivery: The Full Car Shipping Timeline

Total time is two parts: waiting for a carrier to pick up the car, then the drive itself. Here is what happens and how long each step usually takes.

1. **Booking to carrier assigned (usually 1–5 days).** We post your shipment to FMCSA-registered carriers already running your route. Busy interstate routes fill fastest; rural pickups, off-season dates and enclosed trailers can take longer.
2. **Pickup day.** The driver calls ahead with an arrival time. You and the driver inspect the car together and note its condition on the Bill of Lading (BOL), and the car is loaded. This is when transit time starts.
3. **In transit (see the table above).** Drivers often cover about 300–500 miles a day within federal Hours-of-Service limits, with other cars to pick up and drop off along the way.
4. **Delivery.** The driver calls ahead again. You inspect the car against the pickup BOL, sign, and pay any balance due to the carrier. Neon gives you the driver's direct contact for the whole trip.

##  Federal Hours-of-Service (HOS) Regulations & Safety 

For many property-carrying commercial drivers, FMCSA Hours-of-Service rules generally allow up to 11 hours of driving within a 14-hour on-duty window after 10 consecutive hours off duty. Actual miles covered each day vary because of traffic, weather, fuel stops, inspections, loading stops, route conditions, and carrier scheduling. 

These rules are designed to manage driver work and rest periods as part of commercial-vehicle safety requirements. For official federal driver Hours-of-Service information, review the [FMCSA Hours-of-Service Regulations](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-of-service).

## Are Car Shipping Delivery Dates Guaranteed? What FMCSA Rules Say

**No — in auto transport, pickup and delivery dates are estimates.** The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) registers brokers and carriers (their MC and USDOT numbers), requires brokers to hold a $75,000 bond and carriers to carry insurance, and enforces safety rules such as Hours-of-Service. It does not set delivery dates for vehicle shipments. Your dates come from your shipping agreement.

Neon's [terms](/terms/) say this plainly: estimated pickup and delivery dates are approximate and not guaranteed, and that applies to expedited shipping too. Weather, road closures, other customers' pickups and truck breakdowns are outside anyone's control.

### If a company promises a guaranteed date

- Ask what you get if they miss it — a refund, a discount, or nothing — and get it in writing.
- Ask whether the guarantee covers pickup, delivery, or both.
- Be careful with a very low price and an exact delivery date. Carriers can't promise both.

### How to plan around an estimate

- Don't book a flight, lease return or sale for the same day as the estimated delivery.
- Keep a rental car or ride for 2–3 extra days on long routes.
- Ask for the driver's phone number. Neon gives you direct driver contact, so you hear about delays early.

##  Key Factors That Impact Delivery Schedules 

### Weather & Road Conditions

Severe weather, road closures, heavy snow, ice, hurricanes, wildfire conditions, or other safety concerns can delay pickup, transit, or delivery. The carrier prioritizes safe operation and coordinates updated timing when conditions affect the route.

### Traffic & Highway Detours

Metropolitan congestion, construction zones, and accidents can add travel time along major interstate corridors.

### Multi-Vehicle Carrier Stops

Auto carriers may make multiple pickup and delivery stops along a route. Loading, unloading, vehicle inspections, and route changes can affect delivery timing.

### Residential Street Access

If truck access is limited by narrow streets, low-hanging trees, local restrictions, or limited turning space, the carrier and coordinator work with you to select a nearby meeting location. Read details on our [Door-to-Door Car Shipping](/services/door-to-door-car-shipping/) page.

## Times of Year That Slow Car Shipping Down

| When | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| December – February | Snow and ice in the North, Midwest and Rockies slow trucks and can close mountain passes. Allow a few extra days. |
| October – December | Snowbirds ship cars south to Florida and Arizona. Southbound trucks fill up, so book early. |
| April – May | Snowbirds head north again. Trucks leaving Florida are in high demand. |
| June – August | Peak moving and military PCS season. More cars compete for space on popular routes. |
| August – October | Peak hurricane season can stop pickups and deliveries in Florida and along the Gulf Coast. |
| Thanksgiving to New Year | Fewer drivers work the holidays, so pickups can take longer. |

### How to Get Your Car Delivered Faster

- **Give a 2–3 day pickup window.** A flexible window lets a carrier fit your car into a trip that is already going your way.
- **Meet the truck near a highway.** A large lot close to an interstate saves the driver time, especially in towns with narrow streets.
- **Choose open transport.** There are far more open carriers than enclosed ones, so pickups are usually sooner.
- **Book 1–2 weeks ahead** in the busy seasons above.
- **Ask about [expedited shipping](/services/expedited-auto-transport/)** if you need an earlier pickup. It costs more, and the dates are still estimates.

##  Frequently Asked Questions About Car Shipping Timing 

Are car shipping delivery dates guaranteed? +

No. In auto transport, pickup and delivery dates are estimates. FMCSA registers brokers and carriers and enforces safety rules, but it does not set delivery dates; they come from your shipping agreement. Neon's terms state that estimated pickup and delivery dates are approximate and not guaranteed, including for expedited shipping. If a company promises a guaranteed date, ask in writing what you get if it is missed.

How long does it take to ship a car across the country? +

Cross-country car shipping usually takes 7–10 days in transit after pickup; winter storms or remote addresses can push it toward two weeks. Timing varies based on the route, carrier schedule, weather, traffic, loading stops, delivery access, and vehicle requirements. 

How long does it take to ship a car 1,000 miles? +

A 1,000-mile vehicle shipment usually takes about 2–4 days in transit after pickup. The actual timeframe depends on carrier routing, other scheduled stops, traffic, weather, and pickup or delivery access. 

Does transit time include the pickup window? +

No. The pickup window happens before transit begins. Transit starts after the carrier loads the vehicle and completes the pickup inspection on the Bill of Lading. 

Can weather delay vehicle transport? +

Yes. Severe weather, road closures, heavy snow, ice, hurricanes, wildfire conditions, or other safety concerns can delay pickup, transit, or delivery. The carrier prioritizes safe operation and coordinates updated timing when conditions affect the route. 

Does enclosed transport arrive faster? +

Not necessarily. Enclosed transport provides added protection from weather and road exposure, but timing depends on carrier availability, route demand, equipment, pickup dates, and delivery conditions. Enclosed capacity may be more limited on some routes. 

What happens if a carrier cannot access my street? +

The carrier and coordinator work with you to choose a nearby safe, legal, truck-accessible meeting point. This can be a large parking lot, truck stop, public lot, or another suitable location close to your address.
