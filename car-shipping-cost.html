Cost Per Mile Breakdown by Transport Type

Auto transport rates operate on a sliding per-mile scale. Shorter routes have higher per-mile costs due to fixed driver overhead (fuel, toll fees, loading time), whereas long-distance interstate routes offer significantly lower rates per mile.

  • Open Auto Transport: $0.50 to $1.00 per mile. Standard multi-car carriers carrying 7 to 10 vehicles.
  • Enclosed Auto Transport: $0.64 to $2.20 per mile. 40% to 50% premium over open transport for climate and weather protection.
  • Expedited Priority Pickup: 20% to 40% premium over standard rates for guaranteed 24–48 hour pickup dispatch.
  • Motorcycle Shipping: $300 to $800 flat rate depending on distance and crate/tie-down setup.

10 Major Factors That Influence Car Shipping Costs

  1. Total Distance & Route Corridor: Longer distances cost more overall, but interstate freight corridors (e.g. I-95, I-10) reduce per-mile rates.
  2. Vehicle Size, Weight & Modifications: Heavier, taller vehicles consume more fuel and space on multi-car trailers.
  3. Transport Method (Open vs. Enclosed): Open carriers are most economical; enclosed carriers add a 30–40% premium for protection.
  4. Operability Condition: Non-running vehicles that roll and steer require winch loading equipment fees ($150–$300).
  5. Seasonal Demand Windows: Summer peak relocations and winter snowbird migrations increase shipping rates by 20–30%.
  6. Pickup & Delivery Location Accessibility: Metro-to-metro shipments are cheaper than remote rural address pickups requiring extra driver miles.
  7. Fuel Price Fluctuations: Diesel fuel surcharges adjust proportionally with national fuel index averages.
  8. Lead Time & Flexibility: Booking 1–2 weeks in advance yields optimal carrier rates compared to last-minute rush orders.
  9. Expedited Priority Scheduling: Guaranteed 24–48 hour dispatch adds priority dispatch fees.
  10. Insurance Coverage Levels: Every carrier Neon books must carry $500,000 in cargo insurance, at no extra charge to you.

Florida to Michigan Car Shipping Cost (Worked Example)

Shipping a standard sedan from Florida to Michigan — a major interstate corridor of roughly 1,300–1,400 miles — typically costs between $700 and $1,200 on open transport, with delivery completed in 3 to 6 days. Prices rise toward $1,600 in April and May, when snowbirds send their cars north and trucks leaving Florida fill up.

Enclosed transport on the same route runs higher, reflecting the 30–60% enclosed premium. What to Ask Before You Accept a Quote

Two quotes with the same price can end up costing very different amounts. Ask every company these questions:

  • Is the price locked? Some quotes are only estimates that go up once a carrier is assigned. Ask whether the price you're quoted is the price you'll pay.
  • Is there a deposit, and is it refundable? Some brokers take a deposit when you book or when a carrier is assigned. Neon Auto Transport charges no upfront deposit.
  • What does it cost to cancel? Ask for the cancellation terms in writing, before and after a carrier is assigned.
  • Whose insurance covers the car? The carrier's cargo insurance does. Ask for the carrier's certificate before pickup.
  • How long is the pickup window? A quote is only useful if a carrier will actually take it within your dates.

If one quote is far below the others, be careful. Carriers choose which loads to take, and a price well under the going rate often means no carrier picks your car up until the price is raised.

Cheapest Way to Ship a Car

  • Choose Open Transport: Open trailers carry up to 10 cars and cost 30–40% less than enclosed shipping.
  • Book 1–2 Weeks Ahead: Advance booking allows brokers to secure optimal carrier rates.
  • Ship in Late Winter (February): February is historically the lowest demand month with competitive rates.
  • Be Flexible on Dates: Giving a 2–3 day pickup window lets carriers optimize route scheduling.
  • Meet Carrier at Nearby Metro Terminal/Lot: Meeting a car hauler near an interstate highway exit saves rural delivery fees.

Can You Ship a Car on a Train?

Direct Answer: Yes, but only on one specific route: Amtrak's Auto Train runs between Lorton, Virginia and Sanford, Florida, and nowhere else.

If your route is anywhere along that corridor, the Auto Train is a genuine, often cost-competitive option — expect to pay roughly $500–$900 total (vehicle fee plus required passenger fare), with a fast ~17-hour transit.

If your route is anywhere else in the country, including Florida-to-Michigan or California routes, train shipping simply isn't available, and truck-based open or enclosed transport is your only real option. This is worth stating plainly, because generic online guides often mention train shipping without clarifying this strict geographic restriction.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to ship a car across the country?

Shipping a car coast-to-coast (e.g., New York to California or Florida to Washington) costs between $1,200 and $1,800 for open transport and $1,700 to $2,500 for enclosed transport. Transit times range from 7 to 10 days.

What is the cheapest way to ship a car?

The cheapest way is open door-to-door transport booked 1–2 weeks in advance during off-peak months (February or March) with flexible pickup dates.

Can you ship a car on a train?

Only on Amtrak's Auto Train route between Lorton, VA and Sanford, FL. No other train-shipping route exists in the U.S. for personal vehicles.

How much does it cost to ship a car from Florida to Michigan?

Typically $700–$1,200 on open transport for the roughly 1,300–1,400 mile route, rising toward $1,600 in the April–May snowbird season, with delivery in 3–6 days.

What is the cheapest day to ship a car?

There is no single cheapest day of the week — booking 2–4 weeks ahead with a flexible pickup window and shipping during shoulder-season months matters more than the day itself.

Is enclosed transport worth the extra cost?

For classic, exotic, or high-value vehicles, yes — it protects against weather and road debris that open transport does not shield against.
