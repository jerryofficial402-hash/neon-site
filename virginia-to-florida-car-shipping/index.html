Can a Car Be Shipped or Delivered From Another State?

Yes. This is a routine, federally-regulated industry — every legitimate auto transport company or broker operates under FMCSA oversight and holds a DOT and MC number you can verify at fmcsa.dot.gov. Here's how it works, step by step:

  1. Get a quote: Enter pickup and delivery ZIP codes, vehicle details, and preferred dates.
  2. Book with no upfront deposit: A carrier is matched to your route and assigned to your shipment.
  3. Pickup and inspection: The carrier meets you (or the seller) at pickup, documents vehicle condition, and signs the Bill of Lading.
  4. Transit: Your vehicle travels via enclosed or open carrier along the route.
  5. Delivery and inspection: The carrier delivers to your address, you inspect together, and sign Proof of Delivery.

Buying a Car Sight-Unseen Out of State?

  • Get an independent pre-purchase inspection: Hire a local mobile mechanic in the seller's city to inspect the car in person before paying.
  • Confirm title transfer process: Ensure titles are signed and mailed or handed off at pickup.
  • Beware "pay in full before pickup" seller shipper demands: Arranging your own shipping keeps you in control.

Is It Cheaper to Buy a Car in Florida or Virginia?

This depends more on sales tax than sticker price, and the rule that surprises most people: you pay sales tax based on where you register the vehicle, not where you buy it.

  • Virginia's motor vehicle sales and use tax is a flat 4.15% (minimum $75), regardless of locality.
  • Florida's state sales tax on vehicles is 6%, plus a county discretionary surtax that can push the total higher.

So if you're a Virginia resident buying a car in Florida and bringing it home to register, you'll generally pay Virginia's lower 4.15% rate, not Florida's — the purchase location doesn't determine your tax rate, your registration state does. This makes Virginia buyers shopping in Florida a genuinely common and tax-neutral move.

How Long Does It Take?

Most Virginia to Florida shipments complete in 2–4 days of transit, plus a 1–2 day pickup window — so plan on 3–6 days from booking to delivery. Shorter routes (Hampton Roads to north Florida) run on the faster end; longer routes to south Florida from Northern Virginia take closer to 4 days.

Hurricane Season Note: Between June and November, named storms occasionally force carriers to reroute around Florida, adding 1–2 days. Booking with a buffer during peak storm months (August–October) is a good idea.
