Cheap Car Shipping: How to Get a Real Low Price (Without Getting Scammed)

By Shazil Ali • Updated September 2026 • 5 min read

Direct Answer

Is Cheap Car Shipping Safe?

Cheap car shipping is safe when the low price comes from real, controllable factors — open transport, a flexible pickup window, and off-season timing. It becomes a warning sign when a quote sits far below every other quote you've gotten for the same route, because that's the single most common pattern behind car shipping's most damaging scam: bait-and-switch, where a lowball number is used to win your booking and then quietly replaced with a much higher price once your vehicle is scheduled or already picked up.

The short version: there's a real "cheap," and there's a fake "cheap." This guide covers both — how to legitimately lower your price, and exactly how to tell the difference before you book.

What Actually Makes Car Shipping Cheaper

Distance and Route Mechanics

Longer routes cost less per mile than short ones, because fixed costs — positioning the truck, driver time, loading and unloading — are spread over more miles. You can't shorten your route, but understanding this helps set a realistic expectation: a 300-mile shipment often costs more per mile than a 2,000-mile one, which is normal economic efficiency, not a sign you're being overcharged. For full distance breakdowns, review our Car Shipping Cost Guide.

Open Transport vs. Enclosed

Open auto transport is the standard, cost-effective choice for the vast majority of shipments and typically costs 40–60% less than enclosed. Multi-car trailers carry 7–10 vehicles at once, distributing fuel and driver costs. Unless your vehicle's value specifically calls for enclosed protection, open transport is the single biggest legitimate lever you control.

Flexible Pickup Timing

A fixed, non-negotiable pickup date narrows which carriers can take your load, which pushes price up. A flexible 2–5 day pickup window gives your broker more carriers to choose from — and more competition among carriers for your shipment tends to mean a better rate.

Off-Season Booking

Prices move with demand the way flight prices do. Routes into Florida and the Southwest get more expensive in fall as snowbird season ramps up; the reverse happens in spring. Shipping outside your route's peak weeks (such as late winter in February and March), where your timeline allows it, is a legitimate and reliable way to lower cost.

The Cheapest Way to Ship a Car (Practical Checklist)

✓ Choose open transport unless your vehicle specifically needs enclosed protection.
✓ Offer a flexible pickup window (2–5 days) instead of demanding a fixed date.
✓ Ship outside your route's peak season where your timeline allows.
✓ Get quotes from more than one source and compare them against market averages — not just against the lowest number you see.
✓ Have your vehicle accessible at pickup; meeting near a wide road or commercial lot prevents difficult-access terminal fees.

When "Cheap" Becomes a Red Flag

The Bait-and-Switch Pattern

This is the single most common car shipping scam, and it works the same way almost every time: a broker quotes a price well below the market average for your route to win your booking. Once you've committed — sometimes after a deposit, sometimes right at pickup — the price increases, often blamed on "fuel surcharges," a "vehicle size correction," or a vague "route change." The original low number was never real; it existed to get your commitment.

Feature Legitimate Quote Bait-and-Switch Lowball
Deposit Charged only on carrier dispatch Upfront deposit before assignment
Pricing Variance Within 10–15% of market rate 20% to 50% below all competitors
License Verification Active USDOT & MC numbers Unverified or missing credentials
Final Payment Paid to carrier upon delivery Demands full wire/Zelle payment upfront

How to Tell a Real Low Quote from a Lowball Scam

A genuinely competitive quote usually falls within a reasonable range of other quotes for the same route and vehicle — not identical, but in the same neighborhood. A quote that sits 20–50% below everything else you've been offered isn't a bargain; it's the opening move of a bait-and-switch. Before you book, get more than one quote for the same route so you actually have something to compare against — a single quote in isolation gives you no way to tell the difference.

What a Legitimate Deposit Structure Looks Like

A legitimate broker charges nothing for a quote, takes a deposit only once a specific carrier has actually been assigned to your shipment, and collects the remaining balance at delivery — not before pickup. Full payment requested up front, or a deposit requested before any carrier has been named, is one of the clearest signs something is wrong.

How to Verify a Car Shipping Company Before You Book

1
Check the FMCSA SAFER System

Every legitimate broker and carrier has an active USDOT and MC number you can verify directly through FMCSA's public database before you send any money.

2
Ask who the assigned carrier is, and check them separately

The broker and the carrier are two different entities with two different sets of credentials — both should check out.

3
Get the price in writing

A verbal quote with no written confirmation gives you nothing to hold anyone to if the number changes later.

4
Be wary of pressure

"Book now or lose this rate" is a sales tactic, not a real scheduling constraint — legitimate carrier availability doesn't disappear in the next ten minutes.

Cheap Car Shipping FAQs

Is cheap car shipping safe?

It can be — when the lower price comes from real factors like open transport, flexible timing, or off-season booking. It becomes risky when a quote is far below every other quote you've received for the same route, which is the classic setup for a bait-and-switch scam.

Why is my car shipping quote so cheap compared to others?

It could be a genuinely good rate from flexible timing or open transport — or it could be a lowball number designed to win your booking before the price increases later. Compare it against at least one other quote for the same route before deciding which one it is.

What's the cheapest way to ship a car?

Open transport, a flexible pickup window, and off-season timing are the three biggest levers you control. Chasing the single lowest number you can find isn't on this list — it's the most common way people end up with a delayed or bait-and-switched shipment.

How do I know if a car shipping company is legitimate?

Verify their USDOT and MC number through the FMCSA SAFER database, get your price in writing, and be cautious of anyone asking for full payment before pickup or applying pressure to book immediately.

What's a normal deposit for car shipping?

A legitimate broker takes a deposit — typically a few hundred dollars — only after a specific carrier has been assigned to your shipment, with the remaining balance due to the driver at delivery. Full payment requested up front is a red flag, not standard practice.
