Cheap Car Shipping: How to Get a Real Low Price (Without Getting Scammed)

Is Cheap Car Shipping Safe?

Cheap car shipping is safe when the low price comes from real, controllable factors — open transport, a flexible pickup window, and off-season timing. It becomes a warning sign when a quote sits far below every other quote you've gotten for the same route, because that's the single most common pattern behind car shipping's most damaging scam: bait-and-switch, where a lowball number is used to win your booking and then quietly replaced with a much higher price once your vehicle is scheduled or already picked up.

The short version: there's a real "cheap," and there's a fake "cheap." This guide covers both — how to legitimately lower your price, and exactly how to tell the difference before you book.

What Actually Makes Car Shipping Cheaper

1. Distance and Route

Longer routes cost less per mile than short ones, because fixed costs — positioning the truck, driver loading time, and interstate access — are spread over more miles. You can't shorten your route, but understanding this helps set a realistic expectation: a 300-mile shipment often costs more per mile ($1.00–$1.50/mi) than a 2,000-mile one ($0.50–$0.60/mi), which is normal economic efficiency, not a sign you're being overcharged. Check our Car Shipping Cost Guide for full distance breakdowns.

2. Open Transport vs. Enclosed

Open auto transport is the standard, cost-effective choice for the vast majority of vehicles and typically costs 40–60% less than enclosed transport. Multi-car open carriers transport 7 to 10 vehicles at a time, splitting fuel and driver overhead among multiple vehicle owners. Unless your car has exceptional classic, exotic, or collector value requiring climate-sealed protection, choosing open transport is the single biggest legitimate lever you control.

3. Flexible Pickup Timing

A fixed, non-negotiable pickup date narrows which carriers can take your load, requiring expedited scheduling fees. Offering a flexible 2–5 day pickup window gives your broker more carriers to choose from — and more competition among carriers for your shipment tends to produce a better rate without sacrificing safety or service.

4. Off-Season Booking

Auto transport prices fluctuate with seasonal demand just like airline tickets. Routes heading south into Florida, Arizona, and Texas get more expensive in the fall during peak snowbird migration; the reverse occurs in spring. Shipping during off-peak demand windows (like late winter in February and March), where your timeline allows, is a reliable way to secure discount car shipping rates safely.

The Cheapest Way to Ship a Car (Practical Checklist)

If you want to minimize your auto transport expense legitimately, follow these tactical steps before booking:

  • ✓ Choose open transport unless your vehicle specifically requires enclosed protection.
  • ✓ Offer a flexible 2–5 day pickup window instead of demanding a guaranteed same-day pickup.
  • ✓ Ship outside your route's peak season (avoid late June–August and peak snowbird corridors when possible).
  • ✓ Get quotes from 2–3 reputable brokers and compare them against market averages — not just against the lowest standalone number you see.
  • ✓ Ensure clear street access at pickup and delivery; meeting a driver near a wide highway access point or parking lot prevents difficult-access accessorial fees.

When "Cheap" Becomes a Red Flag: Recognizing Scams

⚠️ The Bait-and-Switch Pattern

This is the single most common car shipping scam in the industry. An unscrupulous broker quotes a price well below market averages (e.g. $400 on a $900 lane) to lock in your booking. Once you've committed — often after paying an upfront "reservation fee" or waiting days past your pickup date — the broker informs you that no driver will take the load at that price. The rate suddenly jumps to $1,100, blamed on "unexpected fuel surcharges," a "vehicle weight correction," or "carrier shortages." The original low price was never real; it was simply bait.

🔍 How to Tell a Real Low Quote from a Lowball Scam

A genuinely competitive quote usually falls within 10–15% of other verified quotes for the same route, vehicle size, and schedule. A quote that sits 20% to 50% below every other company isn't a bargain — it is the opening move of a bait-and-switch.

Rule of Thumb: If three licensed brokers quote between $1,100 and $1,300 for your coast-to-coast route, and a fourth quotes $650, that $650 quote is practically guaranteed to result in a price hike, missed pickup dates, or cancellation.

🛡️ What a Legitimate Deposit Structure Looks Like

A legitimate auto transport broker charges $0 upfront to book and will never ask for full payment before your vehicle is picked up. A deposit is only processed after a specific, FMCSA-verified motor carrier has been assigned to your shipment with confirmed dispatch details. The remaining balance is paid directly to the carrier upon safe delivery.

How to Verify a Car Shipping Company Before You Book

1. Check the FMCSA SAFER Database

Every legitimate broker and motor carrier has an active USDOT and MC number. You can verify licensing, active operating authority, and bond status on the public FMCSA SAFER system before paying any money.

2. Check the Assigned Carrier Separately

The broker arranging your move and the motor carrier hauling your car are two separate entities. A transparent broker will provide the assigned carrier's USDOT number and insurance certificate before dispatch.

3. Get Your Price in Writing

Never rely on a verbal phone quote. Demand an itemized written quote detailing pickup/delivery locations, transport type, and confirmation that there are no hidden terminal or fuel surcharges.

4. Be Wary of Artificial Urgency

"Book in the next 10 minutes or this rate expires" is a high-pressure sales tactic. Legitimate carrier availability is determined by real-time dispatch capacity, not artificial countdown timers.

❓ Cheap Car Shipping Frequently Asked Questions

Is cheap car shipping safe?

It can be — when the lower price comes from real factors like open transport, flexible timing, or off-season booking. It becomes risky when a quote is far below every other quote you've received for the same route, which is the classic setup for a bait-and-switch scam.

Why is my car shipping quote so cheap compared to others?

It could be a genuinely good rate from flexible timing or open transport — or it could be a lowball number designed to win your booking before the price increases later. Compare it against at least one other quote for the same route before deciding which one it is.

What's the cheapest way to ship a car?

Open transport, a flexible pickup window, and off-season timing are the three biggest levers you control. Chasing the single lowest number you can find isn't on this list — it's the most common way people end up with a delayed or bait-and-switched shipment.

How do I know if a car shipping company is legitimate?

Verify their USDOT and MC number through the FMCSA SAFER database, get your price in writing, and be cautious of anyone asking for full payment before pickup or applying pressure to book immediately.

What's a normal deposit for car shipping?

A legitimate broker takes a deposit — typically a few hundred dollars — only after a specific carrier has been assigned to your shipment, with the remaining balance due to the driver at delivery. Full payment requested up front is a red flag, not standard practice.
