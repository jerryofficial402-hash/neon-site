Is Cheap Car Shipping Safe?

Cheap car shipping is safe when the low price comes from real, controllable factors — open transport, a flexible pickup window, and off-season timing. It becomes a warning sign when a quote sits far below every other quote you've gotten for the same route, because that's the single most common pattern behind car shipping's most damaging scam: bait-and-switch, where a lowball number is used to win your booking and then quietly replaced with a much higher price once your vehicle is scheduled or already picked up.

The short version: there's a real "cheap," and there's a fake "cheap." This guide covers both — how to legitimately lower your price, and exactly how to tell the difference before you book.

✅ Real "Cheap" (Legitimate Operational Savings) Multi-car trailer efficiency, calendar flexibility, and standard freight corridor optimization.
❌ Fake "Cheap" (Bait-and-Switch Lowball) Unrealistically low quotes posted without an assigned driver, followed by forced price increases.

What Actually Makes Car Shipping Cheaper

Understanding the mechanics behind auto transport rates helps you identify legitimate savings opportunities:

1. Distance and Route Mechanics

Longer routes cost less per mile than short ones, because fixed costs — positioning the truck, driver loading time, and interstate tolls — are spread over more miles. A 300-mile shipment often costs more per mile ($1.00–$1.50/mi) than a 2,000-mile cross-country haul ($0.50–$0.60/mi). Check our Car Shipping Cost Guide for full distance tier data.

2. Open Transport vs. Enclosed

Open auto transport is the standard choice for over 90% of vehicle moves, typically costing 40–60% less than enclosed transport. Multi-car trailers carry 7 to 10 vehicles at a time, splitting overhead among multiple customers. Unless your vehicle specifically requires enclosed protection, open transport is the single biggest legitimate lever you control.

3. Flexible Pickup Timing

A fixed, non-negotiable pickup date narrows which carriers can take your load, which pushes prices up with expedited fees. Offering a flexible 2–5 day pickup window gives your broker more carrier options — and more competition among carriers for your shipment tends to produce a better rate.

4. Off-Season Booking

Auto transport prices move with seasonal demand just like airline tickets. Routes into Florida and the Southwest get more expensive in fall during snowbird season; the reverse occurs in spring. Shipping during off-peak demand windows (like February and March) is a reliable way to lower costs.

The Cheapest Way to Ship a Car (Practical Checklist)

Actionable steps to secure the lowest safe price for your vehicle shipment:

✅ Choose open transport: Standard multi-car carriers save 40–60% over enclosed trailers.
✅ Offer flexible dates: A 2–5 day pickup window lets carriers optimize route capacity.
✅ Ship during off-peak months: Late winter (Feb/Mar) offers lowest annual rates.
✅ Compare 2–3 written quotes: Compare against market averages, not just the lowest standalone bid.
✅ Ensure easy trailer access: Meeting your hauler near a wide highway access point prevents extra fees.

When "Cheap" Becomes a Red Flag

The Bait-and-Switch Pattern

This is the single most common car shipping scam: a broker quotes a price well below the market average to win your booking. Once you've committed — sometimes after a deposit, sometimes right at pickup — the price increases, often blamed on "fuel surcharges," a "vehicle size correction," or a vague "route change." The original low number was never real; it existed solely to get your commitment.

What a Legitimate Deposit Structure Looks Like

A legitimate broker charges $0 upfront to book, takes a deposit only once a specific carrier has actually been assigned to your shipment, and collects the remaining balance at delivery — not before pickup. Full payment requested upfront is one of the clearest signs something is wrong.

How to Verify a Car Shipping Company Before You Book

1. Check the FMCSA SAFER Database

Every legitimate broker and carrier has an active USDOT and MC number you can verify directly on the public FMCSA SAFER system before sending any money.

2. Check the Assigned Carrier Separately

The broker and the carrier are two different entities with two different sets of credentials — ensure your broker confirms the assigned carrier's cargo insurance certificate prior to dispatch.

3. Get the Price in Writing

A verbal quote with no written confirmation gives you nothing to hold anyone to if the number changes later. Always demand an all-inclusive written confirmation.

4. Be Wary of Artificial Sales Pressure

"Book now or lose this rate" is a high-pressure sales tactic, not a real scheduling constraint — legitimate carrier availability doesn't disappear in ten minutes.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is cheap car shipping safe?

It can be — when the lower price comes from real factors like open transport, flexible timing, or off-season booking. It becomes risky when a quote is far below every other quote you've received for the same route, which is the classic setup for a bait-and-switch scam.

Why is my car shipping quote so cheap compared to others?

It could be a genuinely good rate from flexible timing or open transport — or it could be a lowball number designed to win your booking before the price increases later. Compare it against at least one other quote for the same route before deciding which one it is.

What's the cheapest way to ship a car?

Open transport, a flexible pickup window, and off-season timing are the three biggest levers you control. Chasing the single lowest number you can find isn't on this list — it's the most common way people end up with a delayed or bait-and-switched shipment.

How do I know if a car shipping company is legitimate?

Verify their USDOT and MC number through the FMCSA SAFER database, get your price in writing, and be cautious of anyone asking for full payment before pickup or applying pressure to book immediately.

What's a normal deposit for car shipping?

A legitimate broker takes a deposit — typically a few hundred dollars — only after a specific carrier has been assigned to your shipment, with the remaining balance due to the driver at delivery. Full payment requested up front is a red flag, not standard practice.
