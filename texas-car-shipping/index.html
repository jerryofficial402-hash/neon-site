Why Texas Car Shipping Doesn’t Work Like Other States

Texas auto transport has its own logic, and it comes down to three things: size, weather, and the sheer volume of freight and vehicle traffic moving through the state at any given time.

  • The state is bigger than most countries. Houston to El Paso is 745 miles — farther than the drive from Dallas to Chicago. A vehicle shipment that never leaves Texas can still be a multi-day, several-hundred-dollar undertaking.
  • Short hauls cost more per mile, not less. Every car shipment carries a baseline cost for the carrier — fuel, driver time, loading and unloading, insurance overhead — that doesn’t shrink much just because the trip is short. Spread that baseline over 1,500 miles and the per-mile rate looks low even though the total price is higher; concentrate it into 200 miles and the per-mile rate climbs even though the total dollar amount is lower. A Houston-to-Austin quote can work out to $2 a mile while a Houston-to-Los Angeles quote comes in under a dollar a mile — both can be fair, honest pricing.
  • Interstate corridors carry the traffic that keeps prices competitive. I-10, I-20, I-35, I-40, and I-45 all cut across Texas, linking West Coast, Gulf Coast, Midwest, Southeast, and Mexico-border freight lanes. Step off those corridors — into West Texas ranch country, the Permian Basin, or a rural stretch of the Rio Grande Valley — and you’re asking a carrier to go out of their way, which shows up as either a longer wait or a higher price.
  • Texas weather is a real scheduling factor. North Texas gets serious hail storms almost every spring, and the Gulf Coast — Houston in particular — sits squarely in hurricane season from June through November. Both can delay pickups and are good reasons to consider enclosed transport for anything you can’t afford to have sitting exposed on an open trailer during a storm system.
  • Military relocation traffic shapes the calendar. Fort Cavazos near Killeen, Fort Bliss in El Paso, and Joint Base San Antonio all move a steady stream of vehicles tied to PCS orders, and demand spikes hard from May through August. If you’re shipping around a PCS date, book earlier than you think you need to.

What Actually Goes Into Your Texas Car Shipping Quote

  • Distance — with the caveat above: short in-state hops often carry a higher per-mile rate than long cross-country hauls, even though the total price is lower.
  • Vehicle size and weight — Texas has more trucks and full-size SUVs on the road than almost anywhere in the country, and they cost more to ship than a compact sedan.
  • Open vs. enclosed transport — open carriers are the standard, most economical option. Enclosed transport typically runs 40–60% more in Texas, and it’s worth it for classic cars, exotics, low-clearance vehicles, or anything you don’t want exposed to a spring hailstorm or summer dust event.
  • Pickup flexibility — a firm, non-negotiable pickup date is harder for a carrier to plan around than a flexible multi-day window. If your schedule allows any wiggle room, mention it — it’s one of the easiest ways to bring your quote down.
  • Season — PCS season (May–August) and snowbird season (October–December) both tighten carrier supply and can add a modest premium.
  • Running condition — a vehicle that doesn’t start or drive needs to be winched onto the trailer instead of driven on, which typically adds $100–$250 to the quote. Always disclose this upfront.

Houston Car Shipping

Houston is the largest car shipping market in Texas, and its location makes it a genuine logistics hub rather than just a big city — I-10 runs east-west through the metro connecting it to New Orleans and the entire Gulf Coast, while I-45 runs north to Dallas and south to Galveston. That interstate access keeps Houston car shipping quotes on the more competitive end for a metro of its size. The one real wrinkle is hurricane season (June–November), which can tighten carrier availability with little notice — a legitimate reason some Houston shippers choose enclosed transport for a newer or higher-value vehicle. Neon Auto Transport covers the full Houston metro, including Sugar Land, The Woodlands, Katy, and Pearland.

Dallas-Fort Worth Car Shipping

DFW sits at the crossing point of I-20, I-30, I-35, and I-45 — genuinely one of the best-connected metros in the country for auto transport, which is why Dallas car shipping and Fort Worth vehicle transport quotes tend to be some of the most competitive in the state. The metro’s real seasonal consideration is spring hail (March–May) — the single biggest reason DFW shippers choose enclosed transport for a higher-value vehicle during those months, since a hailstorm can total a car’s paint and glass in minutes. Coverage extends across the full metroplex — Plano, Arlington, Irving, Frisco, and Fort Worth proper.

Austin Car Shipping

Austin’s growth over the past decade has been driven by tech company relocations and a wave of newcomers from California, New York, and elsewhere — which means Austin car shipping demand skews heavily toward one-way moves into the city rather than local, short-haul traffic. Its position roughly equidistant from Houston, Dallas, and San Antonio makes it one of the easier Texas Triangle cities to schedule a carrier around. If you’re relocating to Austin for a new job, book two to three weeks ahead of your ideal pickup date — carrier capacity on the Austin leg can tighten with less notice than in Dallas or Houston.

San Antonio Car Shipping

San Antonio is anchored by I-10 and I-35, and it carries a distinct kind of demand compared to the rest of the Texas Triangle: Joint Base San Antonio is one of the largest military medical, training, and personnel hubs in the country, so a significant share of San Antonio vehicle transport is tied directly to PCS orders rather than civilian relocation. Carriers serving San Antonio are generally well-practiced at coordinating around base-access rules and PCS report dates, but PCS season (May–August) is the busiest stretch of the year for this route, so book early.

El Paso Car Shipping

El Paso sits apart from the rest of Texas, both geographically and logistically. It’s connected to the rest of the state by a single major interstate, I-10, and it’s genuinely closer to Phoenix and Albuquerque than it is to Houston or Dallas — a fact that surprises people who think of “Texas” as one contiguous shipping zone. Because of that, El Paso auto transport quotes are often priced more in line with Southwest and West Coast routes than with the rest of the Texas Triangle. El Paso is also home to Fort Bliss, one of the Army’s largest installations, so a meaningful share of shipping demand here is PCS-driven, and as a border city it sees a steady baseline of vehicle logistics tied to cross-border commerce.

Shipping a Car Within Texas

A huge share of Texas auto transport never crosses state lines — it’s Texans moving between Houston, Dallas, Austin, and San Antonio, or shipping a car out to a family member in El Paso or the Rio Grande Valley.

  • Short routes carry higher per-mile rates, for the reasons explained above — a Houston-to-Dallas run can price at $1.50–$2.00 per mile, while a Texas-to-California haul drops to $0.55–$0.90 per mile, even though the total dollar cost is much lower on the short trip.
  • The Houston-Dallas-Austin-San Antonio corridor is the busiest shipping lane in the state, so despite the higher per-mile rate, these routes are usually easy to schedule and often ship within a day or two of booking.
  • Rural and remote pickups — West Texas ranch country, the Permian Basin oil fields, the Rio Grande Valley — sit outside the main carrier corridors, so expect a longer scheduling window and occasionally a modest premium to get a driver out that direction.

Open vs. Enclosed Transport, and When to Choose Which

  • Open carrier transport is what most people picture when they think of car shipping — a multi-level trailer carrying several vehicles at once. It’s the standard, most affordable option for the overwhelming majority of everyday sedans, trucks, and SUVs.
  • Enclosed transport puts your vehicle inside a fully covered trailer, shielded from weather, road debris, and dust. It typically costs 40–60% more than open transport in Texas — worth it given what it protects against here specifically: spring hail in North Texas that can total a car’s exterior in minutes, dust storms in West Texas, and general road grime on longer hauls. It’s the right call for classic cars, exotics, recent restorations, and any low-clearance vehicle.
  • Door-to-door service is the default for most Texas shipments — your driver will come as close to your actual pickup and delivery addresses as a large carrier truck can safely maneuver, though that sometimes means meeting at a nearby parking lot or wide street in tighter urban neighborhoods or HOA subdivisions.

What to Know About Texas Vehicle Registration Before Your Car Arrives

  • Registration deadline — new Texas residents are generally required to register their vehicle with the county tax office within 30 days of establishing residency.
  • Safety and emissions inspection — Texas requires an annual vehicle safety inspection as part of registration. Vehicles registered in several major metro counties — including Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston-Galveston-Brazoria, Austin, and El Paso — also require an emissions test.
  • Non-running vehicles — if your car doesn’t start or drive, tell your transporter before pickup, not at the curb. Winch-loading typically adds $100–$250 to the price.

How to Choose a Reliable Texas Auto Transporter

  • Check the company’s USDOT and MC number on the FMCSA’s public SAFER System — a free, government-run lookup that confirms active operating authority.
  • Ask about cargo insurance. A reputable company will provide documentation without hesitation, especially for a higher-value vehicle.
  • Understand broker vs. carrier. Many companies quoting you are brokers who arrange your shipment through a network of vetted carriers — a legitimate model, but you should know which one you’re working with.
  • Get the Bill of Lading right. Walk around the car with the driver at pickup and note any existing scratches, dents, or wear; do the same at delivery before you sign off.
  • Get more than one quote. A bid dramatically below everything else is usually a sign of an unlicensed reseller lowballing to win the booking, not a legitimate discount.

Texas Vehicle Transport FAQs

How much does it cost to ship a car to or from Texas? +
Most Texas car shipping routes run between $350 and $1,200 for open-carrier transport. Short in-state Triangle routes — Houston, Dallas, Austin, San Antonio — typically run $350 to $550, while cross-country routes to the East Coast run $800 to $1,200. Your exact price depends on your vehicle’s size, the season, and how flexible your pickup date is.
Why do short Texas routes sometimes cost more per mile than long-distance ones? +
Every shipment carries a baseline cost for fuel, driver time, and handling that doesn’t shrink much on a short trip. Spread that same baseline cost over 1,300 miles instead of 200, and the per-mile rate drops sharply even though the total price goes up. That’s why a 200-mile Houston-to-Dallas run can price at $1.50–$2.00 per mile while a 1,300-mile Texas-to-California haul runs $0.55–$0.90 per mile — the total dollar amount is what matters, not the per-mile figure.
How long does it take to ship a car within Texas? +
Most Texas Triangle routes — Houston, Dallas, Austin, San Antonio — deliver within a single day of pickup, since these are among the busiest carrier corridors in the state. Routes to or from El Paso typically take two to three days given the added distance and lower carrier density heading west.
Does hurricane season affect car shipping in Texas? +
Yes, particularly for Houston and other Gulf Coast routes, from June through November. Storms can tighten carrier availability with little warning and delay pickup windows. If you’re shipping during these months, building extra flexibility into your schedule — and considering enclosed transport for a higher-value vehicle — is a smart precaution.
What’s the difference between open and enclosed car transport in Texas? +
Open transport uses a multi-vehicle carrier and is the standard, most affordable choice for the vast majority of vehicles. Enclosed transport uses a fully covered trailer, costs roughly 40–60% more in Texas, and is the better choice for classic cars, exotics, or low-clearance vehicles — especially useful protection against spring hail in North Texas or dust in West Texas.
Do I need to be present for pickup and delivery in Texas? +
Yes. You or someone you authorize needs to be present at both pickup and delivery to walk through the vehicle’s condition with the driver and sign the Bill of Lading — this document is what protects you if any damage dispute comes up later.
Does Neon Auto Transport handle military PCS moves to Fort Cavazos, Fort Bliss, or Joint Base San Antonio? +
Yes. These installations drive a significant share of Texas auto transport demand, especially during PCS season from May through August, and our carrier network is experienced with base-access requirements and PCS report-date scheduling. Book as early as your orders allow — this is the busiest stretch of the year for these routes.
