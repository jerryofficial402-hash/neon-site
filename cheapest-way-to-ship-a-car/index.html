Is There a Single "Cheapest" Car Shipping Company?

Not really — and it's worth being direct about why. Auto transport pricing works like a live marketplace: a broker posts your shipment, and available independent carriers already running your route bid on it.

The "cheapest company" on any affiliate list is really just whichever company happened to get a good quote on the specific route and date that reviewer tested. Ask five brokers for a quote on the same route today and you'll likely get five different numbers, and the ranking could flip completely next month.

What matters far more than brand lists: Is the company licensed (verifiable on FMCSA's SAFER site), is the quote binding or a lowball bait-and-switch, and does pricing stay consistent from booking to delivery?

How to Actually Save Money on Car Shipping

If you want to secure the lowest possible rate for shipping a vehicle, these five concrete levers make the biggest difference:

1
Choose Open Transport Over Enclosed: Open transport is the industry-standard, most affordable method for daily drivers, saving 30–60% compared to enclosed trailers.
2
Book 2 to 4 Weeks Ahead With a Flexible Window: Giving carriers a 3 to 5 day pickup window is the single biggest lever, letting a broker slot your car onto a truck passing through your area.
3
Ship in Shoulder Season: Shipping in spring or autumn avoids peak summer relocation surges and winter snowbird demand spikes.
4
Beware Lowball Bait-and-Switch Quotes: Be cautious of any quote significantly below every other estimate — lowballers often fail to assign a carrier or hike the price right before pickup.
5
Consider Terminal-to-Terminal: If you live near a major auto transport terminal, dropping off or picking up at the terminal can shave cost by avoiding tight residential navigation.

Dealership Car Shipping — Two Very Different Costs

Dealership shipping costs get confused constantly because searchers blur two completely separate transactions:

1. Manufacturer "Destination Fee"

Fixed $1,000–$2,300 fee itemized on every new car's MSRP window sticker. Covers factory-to-dealership transit. Non-negotiable and set by the auto manufacturer — not something a broker controls.

2. Out-of-State Dealer Purchase Shipping

Shipping a car you bought out-of-state directly to your driveway. Arranged via auto transport brokers like Neon, priced by distance, vehicle size, and transport type.

Why Car Shipping Costs What It Does

Auto transport pricing reflects concrete operational costs: distance (longer routes cost more in total but less per mile), vehicle size and weight (SUVs and trucks use more trailer capacity), transport method (open vs enclosed), seasonality, and route corridor demand.

Fuel costs, toll fees, and driver labor are real operating expenses baked into every legitimate carrier quote — not hidden surcharges.

Florida to California Shipping Cost (Worked Example)

Cross-country shipments from Florida to California (~2,700 miles) typically cost between $900 and $1,800 on open transport for a standard sedan, with delivery in 5 to 10 days.

The wide range quoted across the internet ($600 to $2,700+) mostly reflects vehicle size differences, seasonal surges, and lowball initial quotes that get adjusted later — not random pricing swings.

Towing vs. Shipping — Which Is Actually Cheaper?

Towing costs get conflated constantly because searchers confuse three separate options:

1. Renting a Tow Dolly / Trailer (DIY):

Cheapest option for short local moves if you already own a tow-capable truck or SUV. Adds driving stress, wear-and-tear, and fuel costs.

2. Hiring a Professional Tow Truck Company:

Usually more expensive than auto transport for long distances because tow trucks operate per-mile local dispatch, not efficient multi-car long hauls.

3. Auto Transport Brokerage (Car Carrier):

Most cost-effective and safest option for interstate moves over 200 miles because your vehicle shares trailer space with other cars.

Is It Worth Shipping an Older Car?

If shipping costs equal or exceed the car's market value, driving it (if roadworthy) or selling locally may make more sense. Shipping makes sense for older cars with sentimental value or non-running status.

Can You Ship a Car on a Train?

Only on Amtrak's Auto Train (Lorton, VA to Sanford, FL). Train shipping does not exist for cross-country routes like FL to CA.
