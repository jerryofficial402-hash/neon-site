## Quick Answer: Is My Car Insured During Shipping?

Yes. When you ship with Neon Auto Transport, your car is covered by the carrier's cargo insurance from pickup to delivery. **Every carrier we book must carry $500,000 in cargo insurance.** If the carrier damages your car, the carrier's insurance pays for the repair and **the carrier pays any deductible, not you**. ## How Car Shipping Insurance Works

Neon Auto Transport is a licensed auto transport broker (MC 1703787, USDOT 4355879). We don't drive the trucks. We book your shipment with an independent motor carrier, and that carrier is responsible for your car while it is in their care. The carrier's **cargo insurance** is the policy that pays if your vehicle is damaged during loading, transit or unloading.

Federal rules require car haulers to carry at least $750,000 in liability insurance, which covers damage they cause to other people and property on the road. They do not set a minimum for cargo insurance on car transport, and many small carriers carry only $100,000 or $250,000. That's why we set our own rule: **every carrier we book must carry $500,000 in cargo coverage**, enough for a full trailer of cars.

Before we assign your car, we check the carrier's operating authority with the FMCSA and their insurance on file. You can look up any carrier yourself on the FMCSA [SAFER system](https://safer.fmcsa.dot.gov/) using the MC or USDOT number on your dispatch confirmation, and you can ask us for the carrier's certificate of insurance before pickup.

## What Is Covered and What Isn't

### Covered by the carrier's cargo insurance

- Damage caused while loading or unloading your car
- Damage from an accident while your car is on the truck
- Damage caused by the carrier's or driver's negligence, such as a strap or ramp scratching the car
- Loss or theft of the vehicle while the carrier has it, as set out in the carrier's policy

### Not covered

- Personal items left inside the car
- Damage that was already there at pickup and is marked on the Bill of Lading
- Mechanical or electrical problems the carrier didn't cause
- Weather damage like hail on an open carrier is often excluded — choose enclosed transport for high-value cars
- Damage that wasn't noted on the Bill of Lading at delivery

Exact terms depend on each carrier's policy. If you are shipping a classic, exotic or newly restored car, tell us its value when you book so we can match you with a carrier and trailer that fit.

## Who Pays the Deductible?

Not you. Cargo policies have a deductible, but for damage the carrier is responsible for, **the carrier pays the deductible** and their insurer pays the rest of the repair. You don't pay anything out of pocket for covered damage, and you don't have to go through your own insurance first.

## Should You Keep Your Own Car Insurance?

Yes. Keep your personal auto policy active while the car is being shipped. The carrier's cargo insurance is the main coverage for transport damage, but your own policy may help with things the carrier's policy excludes, such as some weather events. A quick call to your insurer before pickup tells you exactly what your policy covers while the car is on a truck.

## How to Protect Your Claim Before Pickup

- **Wash the car** so scratches and dents are easy to see.
- **Take dated photos and a short video** of every side, the roof, wheels and the odometer.
- **Check the pickup inspection on the Bill of Lading** with the driver. Make sure every existing mark is on it before you sign, and keep your copy.
- **Remove personal items**, toll tags and loose accessories. They aren't covered and can cause damage if they move.
- **Note any leaks or mechanical issues** so they aren't mistaken for transport damage.

## What to Do If Your Car Arrives Damaged

1. **Inspect the car with the driver before you sign.** Walk around it in good light and compare it to the pickup report.
2. **Write the damage on the Bill of Lading** next to the driver's signature. This is the most important step — damage that isn't noted at delivery is very hard to claim later.
3. **Take clear photos** of the damage, the whole car and the signed Bill of Lading.
4. **Email us at [info@neonautotransport.com](mailto:info@neonautotransport.com)** with your order details, the Bill of Lading and the photos. We file the claim with the carrier and follow up with them and their insurer for you.
5. **Get a repair estimate** if the carrier or insurer asks for one. The carrier pays the deductible on approved damage.

## Open vs. Enclosed: Which Protects Your Car More?

Both are insured the same way, but they protect the car differently. On an [open carrier](/services/open-auto-transport/) your car is exposed to weather and road dust, just like driving it, and this is how most cars are shipped. An [enclosed carrier](/services/enclosed-auto-transport/) keeps the car covered from rain, hail, sun and road debris, which is why we recommend it for classic, luxury and exotic vehicles.

## Car Shipping Insurance FAQs

### Is my car insured while it's being shipped?

Yes. Your car is covered by the carrier's cargo insurance from pickup to delivery. Every carrier Neon Auto Transport books must carry $500,000 in cargo insurance.

### Do I pay a deductible if my car is damaged?

No. For damage the carrier is responsible for, the carrier pays the deductible and their insurance pays the rest. You don't pay anything for covered damage.

### Does Neon Auto Transport have its own cargo insurance?

Neon is a broker, so your car is insured through the carrier who transports it. We only book carriers with at least $500,000 in cargo insurance and check their authority and insurance before dispatch.

### What if I notice damage after the driver leaves?

Email us right away with photos. Claims are much stronger when damage is written on the Bill of Lading at delivery, so always inspect the car before you sign.

### Are personal items in the car covered?

No. Cargo insurance covers the vehicle, not belongings inside it. Ship the car empty and remove toll tags and loose accessories.

### Do I need to buy extra insurance to ship my car?

Usually not. The carrier's cargo insurance covers transport damage. Keep your own auto policy active, and if your car is very valuable, tell us its value when you book so we can match the right carrier.
