2026 Florida City Auto Transport Guide

Top Cities in Florida for Car Shipping

The Complete 2026 Route, Cost & Auto Transport Guide. A Neon Auto Transport city-by-city guide to shipping a car to or from Miami, Orlando, Tampa, Jacksonville, Fort Lauderdale, St. Petersburg, West Palm Beach, Naples, Sarasota & Fort Myers.

Calculate Your Rate Instantly ← Statewide Florida Hub

Florida is one of the busiest car shipping corridors in the United States. Between snowbirds relocating each winter, military families receiving PCS orders to bases like NAS Jacksonville and MacDill AFB, retirees moving to the Gulf Coast, and dealerships moving inventory up and down I-95 and I-75, thousands of vehicles cross Florida's borders every week.

This guide breaks down the ten most-shipped-to metro areas in the state, real route distances, and exactly how Neon Auto Transport moves vehicles in and out of each one — covering the two major Florida gateway ports (Miami and Jacksonville), the I-4 corridor hub of Orlando and Tampa, and the smaller but high-demand Gulf Coast and Southwest Florida markets.

Statewide Demand Drivers

Why Is Florida a Top 5 State for Car Shipping?

Florida consistently ranks among the top five states for inbound and outbound vehicle shipments in the U.S., driven by five compounding factors: seasonal snowbird migration, heavy military relocation traffic, sustained retirement-driven population growth, two major international shipping ports, and a hurricane season that makes carrier reliability matter more here than almost anywhere else.

🌴

Seasonal Migration

Snowbirds ship cars south in October–December and north again in March–April, creating predictable seasonal rate spikes.

🎖️

Military Relocations

Bases including NAS Jacksonville, MacDill AFB (Tampa), Naval Air Station Pensacola, and Patrick Space Force Base near Orlando generate steady PCS-driven demand.

📈

Retirement & Relocation Growth

Florida has no state income tax and continues to see net inbound migration, especially to Tampa, Orlando, and Southwest Florida.

🚢

Ports & Logistics Hubs

PortMiami and the Port of Jacksonville (JAXPORT) make South and Northeast Florida key points for international vehicle import/export.

⛈️

Extreme Weather Exposure

Hurricane season (June–November) periodically disrupts routes — one reason licensed, insured carriers with real-time tracking matter more in Florida than in most states.

🛡️

City-by-City Breakdowns

Florida's Top 10 Cities for Car Shipping

Each city below is ranked by shipping volume and carrier network density across Neon Auto Transport's Florida lanes. Every profile leads with the one-line answer, then the detail.

1

Miami — South Florida's International Shipping Gateway

Miami is Florida's #1 car shipping market, with the deepest carrier network in the state and transit times of 3–5 days from New York and 6–8 days from Los Angeles. As the home of PortMiami, it's also a major hub for international vehicle export to the Caribbean and Latin America.

Detail Info
Major RoutesI-95, Florida's Turnpike, I-395, US-1
Avg. Transit from New York, NY3–5 days
Avg. Transit from Los Angeles, CA6–8 days
Local Shipping NotePopular for enclosed transport of exotic and luxury vehicles due to the high concentration of dealerships and collectors.
2

Orlando — Central Florida's I-4 Corridor Hub

Orlando is the easiest Florida city to reach quickly, with transit times of 3–4 days from New York, because carriers running the I-4, I-75, and I-95 corridors pass through daily. High population growth and proximity to Orlando International Airport (MCO) make it a top pick for personal moves and dealer-to-dealer transport alike.

Detail Info
Major RoutesI-4, Florida's Turnpike, US-192
Avg. Transit from New York, NY3–4 days
Avg. Transit from Los Angeles, CA5–7 days
Local Shipping NoteCentral location often means slightly lower per-mile rates because carriers can combine Orlando drop-offs with Tampa or Jacksonville legs.
3

Tampa — Gulf Coast's Largest Metro

Tampa Bay anchors Florida's Gulf Coast shipping lanes, with transit times of 3–5 days from New York, driven largely by MacDill AFB's steady military relocation volume and a fast-growing corporate relocation market. St. Petersburg and Clearwater are typically served on the same routes.

Detail Info
Major RoutesI-4, I-75, I-275
Avg. Transit from New York, NY3–5 days
Avg. Transit from Los Angeles, CA6–8 days
Local Shipping NoteStrong terminal-to-terminal option availability if you want to save on door-to-door surcharges.
4

Jacksonville — Florida's Northern Gateway Port

Jacksonville is typically the cheapest and fastest Florida city to reach from the Northeast and Midwest, with transit times of just 2–4 days from New York, thanks to its position on I-95 and I-10 as the first major stop entering Florida from Georgia. JAXPORT also makes it a key city for international auto shipping, and NAS Jacksonville drives heavy PCS-season volume.

Detail Info
Major RoutesI-95, I-10, I-295
Avg. Transit from New York, NY2–4 days
Avg. Transit from Los Angeles, CA5–7 days
Local Shipping NoteTypically the cheapest and fastest Florida city to reach from the Northeast and Midwest thanks to its I-95/I-10 position.
5

Fort Lauderdale — Broward County's Luxury & Yacht Market

Fort Lauderdale, just north of Miami, shares much of Miami-Dade's carrier density and pricing, with transit times of 3–5 days from New York. Its large luxury and exotic vehicle market ties closely to Port Everglades, and many carriers treat Miami and Fort Lauderdale as a single combined lane.

Detail Info
Major RoutesI-95, I-595, Florida's Turnpike
Avg. Transit from New York, NY3–5 days
Avg. Transit from Los Angeles, CA6–8 days
Local Shipping NoteBook alongside Miami quotes — combined lane volume often means better rates and faster pickup windows.
6

St. Petersburg — Tampa Bay's Coastal Twin City

St. Petersburg shares Tampa's carrier lanes, with transit times of 3–5 days from New York, but carries its own retiree-heavy relocation demand plus a growing downtown professional population shipping vehicles for corporate relocations.

Detail Info

7

West Palm Beach — Palm Beach County's Snowbird Capital

West Palm Beach sees some of the highest seasonal snowbird volume of any city in the state, with the fastest-filling lane in Florida every October through December from Northeast markets (NY, NJ, CT, MA). Transit from New York typically runs 3–4 days.

8

Naples — Southwest Florida's Luxury Enclave

Naples has one of the highest concentrations of luxury and collector vehicles per capita in Florida, driving strong demand for enclosed car shipping, with transit from New York typically running 4–6 days. It sits slightly off the main interstate grid, so lead times can run a bit longer than Miami or Tampa.

Sarasota — Gulf Coast Arts & Retirement Hub

Sarasota is a consistent mid-volume lane driven by a steady retiree relocation market, with transit from New York typically running 3–5 days, and carriers frequently combine Sarasota stops with Tampa or Fort Myers runs.

10

Fort Myers — Southwest Florida's Fastest-Growing Metro

Fort Myers has seen some of the fastest population growth in the state over the past decade, with shipping demand growing right alongside it and transit from New York typically running 4–6 days. It's also the natural hub for Naples, Cape Coral, and Bonita Springs shipments.

Pricing Analysis

Car Shipping Cost to Florida: What Drives the Price

Cost to ship a car to Florida ranges from $150 for short in-state moves to $1,700+ for cross-country routes, depending on distance, vehicle type, method, and season. There's no single flat rate — auto transport quotes are built from several variables, outlined below.

6 Factors That Change Your Price

1. Distance & Route Popularity

High-traffic lanes like I-95 (East Coast) are typically cheaper per mile than remote or rural drop-off points.

2. Vehicle Size & Weight

SUVs, trucks, and oversized vehicles cost more than a standard sedan.

3. Open vs. Enclosed Shipping

Enclosed transport runs roughly 40–60% more than open carrier transport, but protects from weather and road debris.

4. Seasonality

Snowbird season (Oct–Dec southbound, Mar–Apr northbound) drives up demand and prices on Florida lanes specifically.

5. Timing Flexibility

Expedited shipping (guaranteed pickup dates) costs more than a flexible pickup window.

6. Vehicle Condition

Shipping non-running cars requires winch-equipped trailers and typically adds a fee.

Consumer Protection & Safety

How to Choose a Trustworthy Florida Auto Transport Company

Florida's high shipping volume unfortunately also attracts scams — low-ball quotes that balloon after booking, brokers with no real carrier network, or unlicensed haulers. Verify these five things before you book with anyone:

1

FMCSA Licensing

Every legitimate carrier and broker must hold an active USDOT and MC number, searchable on the FMCSA's SAFER database.

2

Cargo Insurance Verification

Confirm active insured transport coverage and ask for a certificate of insurance before pickup.

3

Vetted, Background-Checked Haulers

Neon Auto Transport works exclusively with vetted carriers meeting minimum safety-rating and insurance thresholds.

4

Verified Third-Party Reviews

Check Google, BBB, and Transport Reviews, not just testimonials on the company's own site.

5

Transparent, Binding Quotes

A trustworthy quote process locks your price at booking, with no last-minute increases.

What is a Bill of Lading, and why does it matter?

It's the legal document that records your vehicle's condition at pickup and delivery, signed by both you and the driver. It's the single most important piece of paper if a damage dispute ever comes up, so read it before signing — both times.

How Car Shipping Works: Florida Shipping Checklist

Whether you're shipping to Miami or from Jacksonville, the process follows the same six steps:

2

Delivery & Inspection

📋

Car Shipping Insurance Requirements

Federal law requires all interstate auto transport carriers to carry cargo insurance, though coverage minimums vary by carrier size. Always request proof of insured transport coverage before your vehicle is loaded, and confirm whether your own auto policy provides any supplemental coverage during transit.

15 Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently Asked Questions: Shipping a Car to Florida

Common questions about costs, transit windows, insurance, and city-specific logistics when shipping a vehicle to or from Florida.

How much does it cost to ship a car to Florida? +
Average cost ranges from $150 for short in-state moves to $1,700+ for cross-country routes from the West Coast. Get an exact number with a free quote based on your specific pickup and delivery cities.
What is the cheapest way to ship a car to Florida? +
Open car carrier, terminal-to-terminal transport with a flexible pickup window is typically the most budget-friendly option.
How long does it take to ship a car to Miami or Orlando? +
From the Northeast, expect 3–5 days. From the West Coast, expect 6–9 days. In-state moves (e.g., Tampa to Orlando) usually complete within 1–2 days.
Is door-to-door or terminal-to-terminal car shipping better for Florida? +
Door-to-door is more convenient and the most popular option; terminal-to-terminal is a good cost-saving alternative if you're near a major terminal hub like Miami, Tampa, or Jacksonville.
Do I need enclosed shipping for a luxury car in Florida? +
It's not required, but strongly recommended for exotic, luxury, or classic vehicles to protect against road debris, sun exposure, and Florida's frequent summer storms.
What documents do I need for car shipping? +
The bill of lading (provided by the carrier), vehicle registration or title, photo ID, and any applicable power-of-attorney if someone else is dropping off or receiving the vehicle.
Which Florida city is cheapest to ship to? +
Jacksonville typically offers the fastest and most affordable rates from the Northeast and Midwest, thanks to its position directly on I-95 and I-10.
Which Florida city is best for shipping a luxury or exotic car? +
Miami, Fort Lauderdale, and Naples see the highest concentration of enclosed luxury and exotic vehicle shipments in the state.
Can I combine a shipment to two nearby Florida cities to save money? +
Yes. Combined-lane cities like Miami/Fort Lauderdale, Tampa/St. Petersburg, and Naples/Fort Myers often move faster and price better than a single-city request.
Is my vehicle insured during transport? +
Yes. Federal law requires interstate carriers to carry cargo insurance; always confirm active coverage and request a certificate of insurance before pickup.
How do I know if a Florida auto transport company is legitimate? +
Verify their USDOT and MC number on the FMCSA's SAFER database, confirm active cargo insurance, and check third-party reviews on Google, BBB, or Transport Reviews rather than relying only on the company's own site.
Does hurricane season affect Florida car shipping? +
Yes. Storms between June and November can occasionally delay pickup or delivery on Gulf and Atlantic coastal routes; a flexible pickup window helps carriers route around weather.
When is Florida's busiest car shipping season? +
October through December (southbound snowbird arrivals) and March through April (northbound departures) are the busiest and priciest windows — book 2–3 weeks ahead during these months.
Can you ship a non-running car in Florida? +
Yes, with a winch-equipped carrier arranged in advance; disclose the vehicle's condition upfront, as it affects carrier assignment and price.
Do you ship RVs and motorhomes in Florida? +
Yes — demand is especially high from the Fort Myers and Naples seasonal RV communities.
