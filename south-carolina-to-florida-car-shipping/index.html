Quick Answer • Route Snapshot

Shipping a standard sedan from South Carolina to Florida typically costs $400 to $750 on open transport, with enclosed transport running $900–$1,300. The route covers 200–600 miles depending on your exact cities — Charleston to Jacksonville is a short 200-mile hop, while Columbia to Miami runs closer to 600 miles. Transit takes 1–3 days after pickup. One thing worth knowing: this direction is typically 10–15% cheaper than the Florida-to-South-Carolina reverse route, because more trucks run empty southbound than northbound, so carriers price this leg to fill space.

Why People Ship This Route

☀️ Snowbird Migration (The Big One)

Every fall, seasonal residents from Bluffton, Hilton Head, and Myrtle Beach ship vehicles down to Naples, Sarasota, and Palm Beach for the winter — peak volume runs October and November. The reverse trip back north peaks in April and May.

🎖️ Military PCS Moves

South Carolina hosts Fort Jackson (Columbia) and Shaw AFB (Sumter), both of which regularly rotate personnel to Florida installations like MacDill AFB (Tampa), NAS Jacksonville, and NAS Pensacola. May–August is the busiest PCS window on this lane, and rates typically run 10–15% higher during that stretch due to demand.

🌴 Permanent Relocation

Florida's no state income tax continues to pull permanent movers out of South Carolina year-round, independent of season.

🚗 Auction & Dealer Transport

Charleston and Columbia dealer networks move inventory into Florida markets regularly.

How Long Does It Take?

Most South Carolina to Florida shipments complete in 1–3 days of transit, plus a 1–2 day pickup window — so plan on 2–5 days from booking to delivery. This is one of the faster interstate corridors we run since most of it sits on I-95, and short-haul routes like Charleston to Jacksonville can occasionally complete in a single driving day.

Hurricane Season Note: Between June 1 and November 30 (NOAA's official Atlantic hurricane season), carriers may reroute or pause loads for 24–72 hours around an active named storm. If you're booking during August–October, build in a 3–4 day buffer rather than cutting it close.

Open Transport (Standard)

Open transport is the default for this corridor and the right call for the vast majority of shipments — daily drivers, SUVs, trucks. It's faster to schedule since more carriers run open trailers on this lane.

Delivering Into Florida's Gated Communities

A detail most quotes don't mention: a meaningful share of Florida deliveries on this route land inside gated communities, especially in the Naples, Sarasota, and Palm Beach areas that snowbirds from South Carolina favor. Large carrier trucks typically can't get past the gate — your driver will usually meet you at the community entrance or a nearby parking lot to complete the handoff. Worth planning for if your delivery address is in a gated neighborhood.

What to Watch Out For (Common Complaints on This Route)

The two complaints that come up most in customer forums and review threads about interstate car shipping generally, and this snowbird corridor specifically:

  • Lowball quotes that "increase" near pickup: If one quote comes in noticeably below every other quote, it's often a price no carrier will actually accept — expect a call asking for more money close to your pickup date.
  • Deposits taken before a carrier is confirmed: Be cautious of any company asking for full or large payment before a specific carrier is assigned to your shipment.
Neon Auto Transport requires $0 upfront deposit, and the price you're quoted is the price you pay — no calls asking for "just a little more" near pickup.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to ship a car from South Carolina to Florida?

Most shipments cost $400 to $750 on open transport, depending on your exact route and vehicle size. Enclosed transport for classic or luxury vehicles typically runs $900 to $1,300.

How long does it take to ship a car from SC to FL?

Transit typically takes 1 to 3 days, depending on your exact pickup and delivery cities. Add a 1–2 day pickup window for a realistic door-to-door timeline of 2–5 days.

Is South Carolina to Florida cheaper than Florida to South Carolina?

Yes, typically by 10–15%. More carriers run empty trucks southbound than northbound on this corridor, so southbound (SC to FL) pricing tends to run lower to fill that capacity.

Does hurricane season affect this route?

Occasionally. Between June 1 and November 30, carriers may reroute or pause around active named storms, adding 1–2 days. Booking with a buffer during August–October is a good idea, though most shipments in this window are unaffected.

Can I ship my car during a military PCS move from South Carolina to Florida?

Yes. We regularly handle PCS shipments between South Carolina bases (Fort Jackson, Shaw AFB) and Florida bases (MacDill AFB, NAS Jacksonville, NAS Pensacola), including coordination around government paperwork and scheduling.

Will my carrier deliver directly to my address inside a gated community?

Usually not all the way to your door — large carrier trucks typically can't enter gated communities. Your driver will coordinate a meeting point at the entrance or a nearby parking lot, which is standard practice for Naples, Sarasota, and Palm Beach deliveries.

Do you ship non-running vehicles from South Carolina to Florida?

Yes. Non-running vehicles need a carrier with a winch or hydraulic lift gate — let us know when booking so we assign the right equipment.

Is my vehicle insured during transit?

Yes. Every carrier in our network carries active cargo insurance, and Neon Auto Transport provides up to $500,000 in coverage from pickup to delivery.
