California to Texas Auto Transport Route — I-10 Corridor

From Southern California, enclosed carriers head east on Interstate 10 (I-10) through Arizona and New Mexico into West Texas, then fan out to El Paso, San Antonio, Austin, Houston, and up toward Dallas. This is one of the most efficient and heavily trafficked auto transport routes in the country.

Primary Route Details

  • Starting point: Los Angeles, San Diego, San Francisco Bay Area, Sacramento
  • Primary highway: I-10 East (Los Angeles → Phoenix → Tucson → El Paso → San Antonio → Houston)
  • Secondary routes: I-5 South to I-10, I-20 East to Dallas, I-35 North to Austin/Dallas
  • Distance range: 1,400–1,900 miles depending on cities
  • Typical transit time: 3–6 days once picked up

Best Time to Ship from California to Texas

The cheapest time to ship from California to Texas is late fall and winter (January–April), avoiding peak summer relocation season. Late summer (August–October) also offers good pricing and availability, especially for students or snowbirds relocating between the states.

Seasonal Pricing Trends

  • Summer (May–August): Peak relocation season. Book 2–3 weeks ahead and expect prices 10–20% higher.
  • Fall (September–November): Moderate demand, better pricing. Ideal for non-urgent shipments.
  • Winter (December–March): Lowest demand, best pricing. Avoid holiday weeks for fastest pickup.
  • Spring (April–May): Demand picks up as relocation season begins. Prices rise slightly.

Documents Needed to Ship Your Car from California to Texas

For domestic interstate car shipping from California to Texas, the paperwork is minimal. Carriers require one document to move your vehicle: the Bill of Lading (BOL), which doubles as the vehicle condition report.

Required Documents

  • Bill of Lading / Vehicle Condition Report: Always required. Driver issues this at pickup and delivery to document vehicle condition.
  • Carrier's Cargo Insurance Info: Always present. Carriers must be insured; you don't need to show proof of personal insurance.

Optional Documents (Recommended)

  • Vehicle Title or Registration: Rarely required, but some carriers request it if there are financing liens or special scenarios.
  • Proof of Personal Insurance: Optional but recommended for your own risk coverage during transport.
  • Valid Photo ID: May be requested to verify identity but not legally required.
  • Lien Authorization / Power of Attorney: Only required if someone else is handling the transport or the car is financed.

What to Have Ready at Pickup

  • Keys to the vehicle (and spare if available)
  • Alarm disarm code (if equipped)
  • Gas tank at 1/4 full
  • Remove all personal items and accessories
  • Photos of vehicle condition before loading

How to Prepare Your Vehicle for Enclosed Transport — California to Texas

Enclosed transport customers on the California to Texas route typically have higher-value vehicles. These preparation steps apply specifically to them:

Before Pickup

  • Detail and photograph the vehicle extensively — every panel, wheel, undercarriage, and interior. Use timestamps.
  • Document all pre-existing imperfections in writing on the Bill of Lading. Be specific — "hairline scratch, driver door, 3 inches above handle"
  • Disable aftermarket alarm systems and provide the disarm code to your driver
  • Remove all personal items, floor mats, and loose accessories
  • Note any mechanical sensitivities — stiff clutch, sensitive brakes, low-clearance front lip
  • Ensure the gas tank is at 1/4 full
  • For low-clearance vehicles — confirm the carrier has a hydraulic lift gate before the booking is finalized
  • For vehicles with aftermarket exhausts — notify the carrier so they can plan loading positioning
  • Retract or remove aftermarket spoilers if they affect trailer clearance
  • Have your title or registration available at pickup for identity verification

At Pickup

Walk the vehicle with the driver before loading. Review and sign the Bill of Lading together — every noted imperfection should be recorded. Keep your copy. This is your legal protection if any new damage occurs during the California to Texas transit.

At Delivery

Inspect in daylight before signing the delivery BOL. Check paint under direct light. Verify all panels, glass, and wheels. Any new damage must be noted on the BOL before signing and photographed immediately. Do not sign a clean BOL if you have any concerns.

Enclosed Car Shipping California to Texas — FAQs

How much does enclosed car shipping from California to Texas cost?

Enclosed car shipping from California to Texas costs between $1,400 and $2,200 in 2026, depending on vehicle type, route, and season. Luxury sedans average $1,400–$1,900, while SUVs and exotics range $1,600–$2,200.

How long does it take to ship a car from California to Texas?

Enclosed car shipping from California to Texas typically takes 3–6 days in transit, with total time from booking to delivery averaging 7–10 days. Pickup windows are 2–4 days on this high-volume route.

What is the best time to ship a car from California to Texas?

The cheapest time to ship from California to Texas is late fall and winter (January–April), avoiding peak summer relocation season. Late summer (August–October) also offers good pricing and availability.

Do I need insurance to ship my car from California to Texas?

You do not need to show proof of personal insurance to ship your car. Carriers are required to carry cargo insurance. However, having your own insurance active during transport is recommended for added protection.

What documents do I need to ship my car from California to Texas?

The only required document is the Bill of Lading (BOL), which the driver issues at pickup and delivery. Title, registration, and proof of insurance are optional but recommended in certain cases.

Is enclosed transport worth it for California to Texas?

Yes, for luxury, classic, exotic, and high-value vehicles. The 1,500-mile journey crosses desert, mountain, and plains terrain, exposing vehicles to dust, UV radiation, and road debris. Enclosed transport provides complete protection and higher insurance coverage.

Can you ship a non-running vehicle from California to Texas?

Yes. Enclosed carriers can transport inoperable vehicles using winches and hydraulic lift gates. Non-running vehicles may incur an additional fee of $100–$250 depending on the equipment needed to load the vehicle safely.

What vehicles require a hydraulic lift gate for enclosed transport?

Any vehicle with ground clearance under 4 inches requires a lift gate. This includes most modern supercars (Lamborghini Huracán, Ferrari 488, McLaren 720S, Porsche 911 GT3), extensively lowered vehicles, and vehicles with aftermarket front splitters or lips. Always notify us of your vehicle's ground clearance at booking so we confirm lift gate availability before dispatch.
