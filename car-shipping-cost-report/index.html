Car Shipping Cost Report 2026: Prices on America's Most-Searched Routes

Key Findings

  • New York to California is the most-searched car shipping route in the US, with about 2,600 Google searches a month — twice as many as the next route, Texas to California.
  • Cross-country shipping costs $1,150–$1,800 for a sedan on an open carrier; a 1,300–1,500-mile trip costs about $850–$1,300.
  • Long trips are cheaper per mile: about $0.53 a mile coast to coast, versus $1.15–$1.30 a mile on trips under 400 miles.
  • Enclosed transport costs about 1.4–1.5 times as much as open — usually $500–$700 more on a long route.
  • Quotes for the same route can differ by more than $1,000. On Michigan to California, published prices ranged from $800 to $2,781.
  • Season can move the price by about $300–$450 on snowbird routes between New York and Florida.

Longer Trips Cost Less per Mile

The total price goes up with distance, but the price per mile goes down. Every trip has fixed costs (loading, pickup and delivery in cities, the driver's time) that are spread over more miles on a long route.

That's why a 270-mile trip can cost a quarter of a 2,800-mile trip even though it's only a tenth of the distance. Per-mile prices are the middle of each open-carrier range divided by the driving distance.

Part of the gap is real: different cities, vehicle sizes and dates. But some low prices are bait. A quote well below the going rate often sits on the load board with no carrier willing to take it, and the price goes up later. Before you book, ask:

  • Is this the total price, including fuel, insurance and door-to-door delivery?
  • How much is due upfront, and is it refundable if no carrier is assigned?
  • What happens to the price if no carrier accepts it within a few days?

Season Matters Most on Snowbird Routes

Between New York and Florida, the price depends on which way the snowbirds are moving. Trucks fill up heading south in the fall and heading north in the spring, and the empty direction gets cheaper.

Seasonal car shipping prices between New York and Florida
Direction Busy season Quiet season
New York → FloridaOct–Jan: $1,050–$1,450Mar–May: $750–$1,000
Florida → New YorkMar–May: $1,000–$1,450Oct–Jan: $800–$1,000

Other companies see the same pattern; one published a northbound price of $749 against $1,049 southbound. If your dates are flexible, shipping against the snowbird flow can save $300–$450. See our snowbird car shipping guide.

Open vs. Enclosed: the Real Premium

On every route in this report, enclosed transport cost about 1.4–1.5 times the open-carrier price, roughly $400–$550 more on a 1,300-mile trip and $500–$700 more coast to coast. Enclosed is worth it for classic, exotic and new cars, or to keep winter road salt off a car shipped from the Midwest. For a daily driver, open transport is how most cars travel. How We Put This Report Together

  • Route prices: Neon Auto Transport's 2026 price estimates for a running sedan with a flexible pickup window. Distances are approximate driving miles between the example cities.
  • Published prices: prices and averages shown on other companies' and price sites' public pages, collected November 22–24, 2026. Some are for specific city pairs, as noted. They change often.
  • Season prices: Neon's estimates for the New York–Florida route by month, plus a competitor's published northbound and southbound prices.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to ship a car across the country in 2026?

About $1,150–$1,800 for a sedan on an open carrier and $1,750–$2,500 enclosed, for trips of 2,300–2,900 miles such as New York or Florida to California.

What is the most popular car shipping route?

By Google searches, New York to California, with about 2,600 searches a month, followed by Texas to California with about 1,300.

How much does car shipping cost per mile?

On an open carrier, about $0.50–$0.60 a mile on cross-country trips, about $0.80 a mile on 1,300–1,500-mile trips, and $1.15–$1.30 a mile on trips under 400 miles.

Why are car shipping quotes so different?

Quotes differ by city, vehicle, date and service, but some low quotes are set below what carriers will accept and go up later. Ask whether the price is the total and what happens if no carrier takes it.

How much more does enclosed transport cost?

About 1.4–1.5 times the open-carrier price on the routes in this report, usually $400–$700 more.
