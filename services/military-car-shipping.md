## Does the Military Pay to Ship Your Car on a PCS Move?

It depends on where you're going. Ask your installation's transportation office (PPPO) to confirm what your orders cover, but in most cases:

- **PCS within the continental US (CONUS):** the military usually expects you to drive your car and reimburses mileage and per diem for the trip. It generally does not pay to ship a car between two US bases, so if you can't or don't want to drive it, you pay for shipping yourself. Many families drive one car and ship the second.
- **PCS overseas (OCONUS):** the government ships one privately owned vehicle (POV) for authorized moves, but only from a Vehicle Processing Center (VPC). Getting the car to the VPC, or home from the VPC when you return, is up to you. We can truck it to or from the VPC for you.
- **Moving to Alaska or Hawaii:** check your orders first. If your car isn't covered, we arrange the truck leg to the port and the ocean shipment. See [Hawaii](/services/hawaii-auto-transport/) and [Alaska](/services/alaska-auto-transport/) car shipping.

## PCS Car Shipping Checklist

- **Book when your orders arrive.** Two to three weeks ahead is enough most of the year; give yourself more time in summer.
- **Plan around your report date.** Ship the car early enough that it arrives when you do, or pick a delivery date after you've moved into housing.
- **Name someone to release or receive the car** if you'll already be gone. They need to be 18 or older and sign the inspection report.
- **Keep registration and insurance active** during transport, and bring your ID and keys to pickup.
- **Empty the car** of personal items, base decals you no longer need, and toll transponders, and leave about a quarter tank of fuel.
- **Going overseas?** VPCs have their own rules for cleaning, fuel level and paperwork. Check them before we deliver the car to the VPC.

## Tips & Tricks for Saving on Vehicle Shipping

### Pro Tip for Military Car Shipping

Have your PCS orders ready, as some shipments may be partially reimbursable.

### Flexible Pickup & Delivery

Choosing a five-day window for pickup and delivery can lower your shipping cost. Carriers can plan routes more easily with flexible dates, which means more options and better pricing for you.

### Hub City Advantages

For your auto transport needs, choose a location near major highways. Spots close to major interstates attract more carriers, giving you more options and more competitive rates on your shipment.

### Open Trailers

Open trailers cost less than enclosed trailers and work well for most standard vehicles. If your car doesn’t need extra protection from the elements, an open trailer is the smart, budget-friendly choice.

## Factors that Affect Military Car Shipping Costs

### Distance

The farther your car travels, the more you’ll pay. However, longer trips often cost less per mile than shorter ones.

### Route

Shipping along major highways is cheaper and faster. Rural or off-the-beaten-path routes cost more due to limited carrier access.

### Season

Summer and snowbird season drive prices up. Shipping during slower months, like fall or early spring, can save you money.

### Vehicle Type

Larger vehicles like SUVs and trucks take up more space and use more fuel. Expect to pay more than you would for a standard sedan.

### Traffic Patterns

Busy travel weeks, like Thanksgiving, can slow down delivery times. Booking during lighter traffic windows helps keep costs and delays down.

### Carrier Availability

During peak season, carriers fill up fast. You may wait longer for pickup, so book early to lock in your preferred dates.

### Open vs Enclosed

Open trailers are the most affordable option. Enclosed trailers offer extra protection but cost more — ideal for luxury or classic cars.

### Population Density

Shipping to or from a busy city can cost more. Dense urban areas have more traffic and tighter access for large carriers.
