## Why More Families Are Choosing to Ship Instead of Drive

For a first-year student — or a parent already juggling move-in logistics, dorm shopping, and orientation schedules — driving the car personally often isn't practical. Here's what tips the scale toward shipping:

  * **Time savings.** A cross-country drive can eat up 3–5 days including rest stops.
  * **Reduced mileage and wear.** Long highway drives add thousands of miles to a car's odometer.
  * **Safety.** Inexperienced or fatigued young drivers face higher accident risk than professional carriers.
  * **Two-driver problem, solved.** Shipping removes the headache of bringing the second vehicle back.

## Open vs. Enclosed Transport

When shipping to college, you have two primary carrier options:

### Open Auto Transport

The most popular and cost-effective method. Your car is loaded onto an open-air trailer (similar to how new cars are delivered to dealerships). It is safe, but exposed to the elements and road dust.

### Enclosed Auto Transport

Your car is shipped in a fully enclosed trailer with hard or soft sides, protecting it from weather, rocks, and debris. This costs roughly 40–60% more, making it ideal only for high-value or classic cars.

Verdict for Students: Open transport is the standard choice for 95% of college shipments.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

  * **Packing the Car Full of Dorm Stuff** Carriers are licensed for vehicles, not household goods. You can usually place up to 100 lbs of soft items (like clothes or bedding) in the trunk, but the back seat and front seat must be clear.
  * **Booking Too Late** If you need the car picked up on exactly August 15th, don't wait until August 12th to book. Give the broker at least 2 weeks' notice to secure a top-rated carrier.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to drive or ship a car to college? 

When you factor in gas, food, lodging, wear-and-tear, and a second driver's time, shipping is often comparable in cost — and far less stressful — for distances over 500 miles. 

Can I track my student's car during transport? 

Yes! You will receive the driver's direct phone number upon dispatch, allowing you to call or text for ETA updates at any time. 

Do I have to be present at pickup or delivery? 

Either you, the student, or a trusted representative (like a friend, relative, or roommate over 18) must be present to hand over the keys and sign the Bill of Lading inspection report. 

Can a carrier deliver directly to a college dorm? 

Yes, if the campus streets allow an 80-foot commercial truck to safely maneuver. If the dorm is on a narrow or restricted campus road, the driver will ask the student to meet them at a nearby grocery store parking lot or wider street. 

Who is responsible if the car gets damaged? 

The motor carrier's cargo insurance is primary. Any damage that occurs during transport (which is rare) will be covered at no cost to you, provided it is properly documented on the Bill of Lading at delivery.
