##  Why Maryland-to-Florida Is a High-Volume Corridor 

### ☀️ Snowbird Migration

DC-metro and Baltimore-area retirees ship vehicles south for the winter, with the heaviest southbound volume from **January through March** — enough that it's known to cause noticeable I-95 congestion through Virginia and the Carolinas during that window. 

### 🌱 Spring Return Shipments

A pattern specific to this corridor: a large share of Maryland/DC-area residents who relocate to Florida for part of the year ship their car back home each spring rather than drive the ~1,000 miles themselves. It's often cheaper than two days of gas and hotels, and puts zero extra miles on the car. 

### 🎖️ Military & Federal Moves

Maryland is home to Fort Meade (NSA/Cyber Command) and sits next to Joint Base Andrews — both regularly rotate personnel toward Florida installations like MacDill AFB, NAS Jacksonville, and NAS Pensacola. 

### 🚗 Baltimore Auctions & Federal Relocations

Baltimore-area auto auctions move regular volume into Florida's large retail resale market. Additionally, the DC-Baltimore corridor sends a steady year-round stream of federal transfers to Florida.

## How to Ship a Car to Florida

This section answers the general "how do I actually do this" question, whether you're shipping from Maryland or anywhere else: 

  1. **Get a quote:** Enter your pickup and delivery ZIP codes, vehicle make/model/year, and preferred dates.
  2. **Book with no upfront deposit:** A carrier is matched to your route — on high-volume lanes like Maryland to Florida, this typically happens within 24–48 hours.
  3. **Pickup:** The carrier calls ahead, arrives at your pickup location, and you complete a walk-around inspection together before signing the Bill of Lading.
  4. **Transit:** Your vehicle travels via open or enclosed carrier, generally along I-95.
  5. **Delivery:** The carrier delivers to your Florida address, you inspect the vehicle together, and sign the Proof of Delivery to complete the shipment.



If your delivery address is a Florida location the carrier can't physically reach — a gated community, a downtown area with truck restrictions — your driver will coordinate a nearby meeting point in advance rather than surprise you with it on delivery day.
